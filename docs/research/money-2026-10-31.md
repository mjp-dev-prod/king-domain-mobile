# Money: currency, international clients, fees, premium and tax (research, 2026-10-31)

**Why:** The 24 Oct meeting asked for "currency consideration" and international jobs; the discovery and
messaging decisions introduced premium features (D21, D28); real-money testing is planned before the end of
December. PRD research topic 9. It proposes; decisions are in §9. Several of these are money rules and go to
the ledger.

Tags: 🟢 fact (named source) · 🔵 observed (this codebase) · 🟣 synthesis · 🟡 hypothesis · 🔴 unknown.

## 1. Short answer

🟣 A job is priced in **naira or US dollars**, the only two currencies Paystack lets a Nigerian business
charge in. International clients pay dollars by card; the money is held as dollars; **talents are paid in
naira** at the day's rate, shown before they agree, because Paystack can only pay out naira in Nigeria. Every
user can also choose a **display currency** for rough reference. Talents keep 100% of the price; the client's
10% fee pays for everything, including Paystack's charges, which the worked examples below show it covers.
**Premium** must go through Google Play Billing (15%) and must never buy a better position than other
talents. Tax: a small company is exempt today, but how the marketplace's turnover is counted needs an
accountant before real money moves.

## 2. What King Domain has today (🔵)

- Everything in naira (`formatNaira`); Paystack checkout in NGN; payouts by Paystack transfer to a Nigerian
  bank account (`nuban`).
- Fee: the client pays the budget plus a **10% platform fee**, frozen at award; only the budget goes to the
  talent; the fee is never refunded (ledger).
- Known open item (2026-10-24): a written answer from Paystack on holding funds in the Balance (escrow) is
  still needed.

## 3. What Paystack can do (🟢)

- **Charging:** a Nigerian business can charge in **NGN or USD** only. International cards are charged and
  settled **in naira by default** (the customer's bank converts), or in **USD paid out to a Zenith Bank USD
  domiciliary account**. International payments need the business activated and a request approved (about
  48 working hours). Web design and development businesses get extra scrutiny (a valid website).
- **Fees:** local cards and USSD **1.5% + ₦100, capped at ₦2,000** (₦100 waived under ₦2,500); international
  cards **3.9% + ₦100** in naira or **3.9%** in USD; dedicated virtual accounts (bank transfer) 1% capped at
  ₦300. Settlement next working day.
- **Payouts (Transfers):** Registered businesses only. In Nigeria **naira only** (`nuban`); **₦10** up to
  ₦5,000, **₦25** up to ₦50,000, **₦50** above; plus **₦50 stamp duty** on transfers of ₦10,000 or more;
  minimum ₦50, maximum ₦10 million per transfer. **No USD payouts to Nigerian individuals.**

## 4. How Nigerian talents receive dollars today (🟢)

- Fintech USD accounts with US details: **Grey** (ACH 0.8%, min $2, max $10; conversion 1% capped at $6;
  ₦35 withdrawal), **Raenest** (4 free deposits a month, then $1 per ACH; conversion 0.5–0.7% capped at
  $2.70), **Cleva** (ACH 0.8% capped at $5; no separate conversion fee stated); **Payoneer** (about 1% to
  receive, 1.2–4% to convert); bank domiciliary accounts for SWIFT wires (expensive, $80–105 per wire in one
  estimate). Effective total cost on $1,000 ranges from about 0.3% to 5% (Paid Across, 2026).
- Reported, not verified from the circular: from 1 May 2026 the CBN requires money-transfer operators to pay
  inbound retail remittances **in naira** (Techeconomy via Pryme). This concerns remittance routes, not card
  payments to a business, but it shows the direction of FX rules.
- 🔴 Paying talents in dollars would need a licensed partner that can pay into their USD accounts. Not
  researched yet; it belongs to the build phase.

## 5. Proposal: currency (🟣, for the PRD)

- **Price currency per job: NGN or USD.** Local clients price in naira. International clients price in
  dollars and pay by card (3.9%), settled into King Domain's Zenith USD account.
- **Held in the currency paid.** Refunds go back in that currency.
- **Talents are paid in naira**, into their own account (D12), converted at the actual rate on the day of
  payout. Before applying to a dollar job and again before payout, the talent sees the dollar price, today's
  naira estimate, the rate and its source. Paying talents in dollars is added once a partner is in place.
- **Display currency**: everyone can pick a currency to see approximate equivalents next to real prices,
  clearly labelled "about". This is the "currency selection" task from the 24 Oct meeting, now designed
  into the whole flow.
- **International clients' identity**: NIN doesn't apply; they verify with a passport and live selfie or a
  business registry check through the same kind of provider (D16).
- **Prerequisites** (all after CAC): Paystack Registered status, international payments approved, a Zenith
  USD domiciliary account linked.

## 6. Proposal: fees (🟣)

The client pays the price plus 10%; the talent receives 100% of the price. Paystack's costs come out of the
10%. Worked examples (start-proof share of 20% means two transfers per job):

| | ₦50,000 local job (card) | ₦50,000 local job (bank transfer) | $100 international job (USD card) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Client pays | ₦55,000 | ₦55,000 | $110 |
| Paystack charge | 1.5% + ₦100 = ₦925 | 1% capped = ₦300 | 3.9% = $4.29 |
| Two transfers to the talent (₦10,000 + ₦40,000) | ₦25 + ₦25 | same | in naira after conversion, same bands |
| Stamp duty on those transfers | ₦50 + ₦50 | same | same |
| **King Domain keeps** | **₦3,925 (7.9% of the price)** | **₦4,550 (9.1%)** | **about $5.71 before conversion costs** |

Fee per unit: 10% on each funded milestone, month, week of hours, or package. The never-refunded rule stays
as agreed.

## 7. Proposal: premium (🟣)

- **Google Play's rule** (🟢): premium features and subscriptions inside an Android app must use **Google
  Play Billing**; the fee outside the US, UK, EEA, Japan and Australia is **15%** (subscriptions 15%).
  Alternative billing is not offered in Nigeria. 🔵 Upwork and Fiverr take job payments through their own
  systems; 🔴 confirm in the build phase that job payments (services by people) stay outside Play Billing.
- **One rule for every premium feature: it never buys a better place than another talent** (no boosted
  applications, no higher position in browsing), consistent with the no-score and recently-active decisions
  (correction-talent-discovery, D23) and with the evidence that paid boosts are how Upwork monetises
  congestion (discovery §5).
- **Candidates** (to price and confirm in the ledger):
  - *Clients:* reopen a job past 50 applicants (D21); invite more than 20 talents per job; team seats for
    companies (several people managing jobs); a faster "find someone for me" promise.
  - *Talents:* ask a client a question before applying (D28), with the answer added to the job for every
    applicant so information is never paywalled; more package tiers or skills listed at once.
- **The anti-scam message stays exact:** "Nobody on King Domain will ever ask you for money to get or do a
  job. Premium is optional and is only bought inside the app through Google Play."

## 8. Tax and structure (🟢, with an accountant to confirm)

- The **Nigeria Tax Act 2025** took effect 1 January 2026. VAT stays at **7.5%**.
- **Small companies** (turnover up to **₦100 million** and fixed assets up to ₦250 million, excluding
  professional services) pay **0% company income tax** and are **exempt from registering for, charging and
  filing VAT**, unless they opt in (Deloitte; Forvis Mazars; BDO). EY's summary of section 56 gives ₦50
  million: sources disagree, so the threshold needs confirming.
- **Digital platforms or intermediaries earning commissions** are required to collect VAT on the full supply
  value through their commissions (EY's highlights; Deloitte frames it for non-resident companies). 🔴 Whether
  and when this applies to King Domain, and whether turnover means the 10% fees or the full job values,
  depends on how the terms describe King Domain's role (agent holding the client's money vs seller of the
  service). An accountant should answer this before the real-money test.
- Talents' own taxes are theirs; King Domain can give each talent a yearly earnings statement.

### Answered by Victor, 2026-10-31 (carry into the PRD as decisions)

- **D37. Currencies adopted** as in §5: naira or dollar prices, dollars by card into a Zenith USD account,
  talents paid in naira at the day's actual rate shown in advance, a display currency for everyone, dollar
  payouts once a partner exists.
- **D38. Premium never buys a better place than another talent**, and answers to pre-apply questions are
  shared with every applicant.
- **D39. Premium starts with two features**: reopening a job past 50 applicants (clients) and asking a
  question before applying (talents). Prices go to the ledger; more premium features only "way later, when
  we are settled".
- **D40. Tax goes to the team meeting.** Victor will raise it on Saturday. In plain words, for the meeting:
  1. Under the new tax law, a small company (turnover up to ₦100 million, or ₦50 million in one summary)
     pays no company tax and doesn't have to charge VAT. King Domain is small today.
  2. The open question is what counts as King Domain's "turnover": only our 10% fees, or the full value of
     every job that passes through us. If it is the full value, we cross the limit much sooner.
  3. The law also says platforms that earn commissions may have to collect 7.5% VAT on the whole job. Whether
     that applies to us depends on how our terms describe our role.
  4. So: before real money moves in December, ask an accountant those two questions.

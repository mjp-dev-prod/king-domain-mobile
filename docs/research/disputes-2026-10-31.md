# Disputes, refunds and chargebacks (research, 2026-10-31)

**Why:** Stage 3 of the payment rules (disputes, an admin screen, cancel and refund) is not built, and until
it is, auto-release must stay off (a client who dislikes the work would otherwise be charged on silence).
The new job types, the start proof and card chargebacks add cases the ledger text doesn't cover. PRD research
topic 8. It proposes; decisions are in §9.

Tags: 🟢 fact (named source) · 🔵 observed (this codebase) · 🟣 synthesis · 🟡 hypothesis · 🔴 unknown.

## 1. Short answer

🟣 Most disputes should end without anyone from King Domain deciding them. A **staircase**: the app helps the
two sides name the problem and offers fixed ways out (more time, one more revision, a split, a mutual
cancel), each with a clock; either side can escalate at any point; King Domain decides the rest **for free,
within 5 days, with written reasons**, using the record the product already keeps. Card chargebacks are a
separate risk with a 16-hour deadline and need their own handling.

## 2. What is already agreed (🔵, ledger milestone 04, Victor 2026-10-01)

Either side may file within 3 days of the trigger; an admin rules within 5 days; a ruling may be a
percentage split; repeat losing filers are flagged; every delivery is an immutable dated version; rulings are
logged with who and why; the 10% platform fee is never refunded; Paystack keeps its own charge on refunds;
partial refunds are possible and take 3–10 working days to reach the client. After change round 2, or when a
talent misses the resubmit clock, the contract escalates to an admin (`disputed`). Three days past the
delivery date with nothing delivered and no extension, the client may cancel for a full refund of the budget.
**Not built:** the dispute record, the admin screen, rulings, cancel and refund. Auto-release is off until
they exist. Not specified, and not to be invented: any appeal or "final ruling" wording.

## 3. How the big platforms resolve disputes (🟢)

- **eBay / PayPal:** over 60 million disputes a year, **more than 90% resolved without a person deciding**.
  The design is a staircase: problem diagnosis, then direct negotiation assisted by software, then a decision
  by eBay or PayPal; **each party can move it to the next step on their own**, so nobody can stall; and the
  platform calms problems before they become disputes (telling a worried buyer the usual delivery time)
  (Colin Rule, eBay's first head of dispute resolution, 2017; Habuka & Rule 2017).
- **Upwork (fixed price):** the freelancer files; the client has **5 days** to respond, and **silence releases
  the money to the freelancer**; if the client declines, an agent gives a non-binding recommendation within
  about 2 days; both have 2 days to accept; otherwise **paid arbitration**, fee split between the two sides.
- **Fiverr:** a Resolution Center to agree extra time, extra revisions or a mutual cancellation before
  support gets involved; an unanswered extension is accepted after 2 days.
- **The cost problem** (September research): an Upwork arbitration demanded **$675 to recover $75**; a
  Freelancer.com admin released $3,000 to a freelancer who did nothing, with no buyer input. Small jobs need
  free, fast, human decisions with reasons.

## 4. Card chargebacks (🟢 Paystack)

- A client can ask their bank to reverse a card payment. **Nigerian businesses have 16 hours (business days
  only) to respond**, or the chargeback is **accepted automatically** and the full amount is taken from the
  next payout; once accepted it **can't be reversed**.
- Declining needs evidence: a receipt or invoice with **the customer's name as on their ID, the amount, the
  card's first six and last four digits, the date paid, and a description of the service**, plus service
  usage or delivery proof. **Text messages and emails are not enough.** A weak response can come back as a
  pre-arbitration case with 24 hours to answer.
- Paystack says the quality of the receipt depends on the business's own identity checks, sends webhooks
  (`charge.dispute.create`, reminders every 4 hours) and offers an API to answer disputes automatically.
- 🟣 The risk for King Domain: a client pays by card, the talent is paid out on approval, then the client
  charges back. Bank transfers carry far less of this risk.

## 5. Proposal: the dispute staircase (🟣, for the PRD)

### 5.1 Prevent
Deadlines, reminders and the structured brief already prevent many disputes. Add: when a delivery is near its
deadline or a review is close to auto-approval, both sides see plainly what happens next.

### 5.2 "Something's wrong" (diagnosis)
A button in the contract room. The person picks what is wrong: not delivered, not what the brief said,
quality, the other side has gone silent, the client wants more than was agreed, a payment problem. The app
shows the rule that applies (with the brief and the evidence side by side) and the ways out.

### 5.3 Fixed ways out (assisted negotiation)
Either side proposes one: **more time**, **one more revision**, **a split** (a percentage of the held money
to each), **cancel by agreement** (full refund or a split), or **carry on as is**. The other side accepts,
declines or counters within **48 hours**; silence moves it to the next step. Accepted outcomes take effect at
once (money moves, clocks reset).

### 5.4 King Domain decides
- Either side can escalate at any point, and some cases escalate by themselves (after change round 2, a
  missed resubmit clock, an unanswered proposal).
- **Free for both sides.**
- The admin sees one assembled case: the brief, the conversation (undeletable, D27), confirmed call
  summaries (D26), the start proof, every dated delivery version, change requests and their reasons. An AI
  model can lay out the timeline; **a person decides** (NDPA s.27: people must be told about automated
  decisions).
- Where the question is quality in a skill, the admin asks a reviewer who practises it (as in D7).
- Ruling within **5 days**: release, refund or a percentage split, with **written reasons that cite the brief
  and the evidence**, shown to both sides. Logged with who and why.
- Outcomes feed the record: lost disputes count against Proven (D32) and the client track record (D19);
  repeat losing filers are flagged.

### 5.5 Per job type
- **Milestones:** disputes are per milestone; approved milestones are never reopened.
- **Monthly:** per month's deliveries; ending the arrangement follows the agreed notice.
- **Hourly:** per weekly report; hours reported with a description and files are the evidence.
- **Start proof:** a client objection goes to an admin who checks it against the skill's checklist (D8).

### 5.6 Chargebacks
- Every payment produces a **proper receipt** with exactly the fields Paystack asks for, plus the job's
  approval record and delivered files, ready before any chargeback arrives.
- King Domain listens for Paystack's dispute webhooks, alerts the team at once (16-hour clock), and answers
  through the Dispute API with that receipt and evidence.
- The client's identity check (D16) is what makes the receipt credible.
- A client who charges back after approving delivered work is removed. Who absorbs a lost chargeback is
  decision 4.

## 6. What this changes in the app

The "Something's wrong" flow, proposals with clocks, the escalation path, the admin case screen with the
assembled evidence and the ruling form, refunds and splits through Paystack, receipts per payment, the
chargeback alert and response, the record updates, and switching auto-release on once all of this is live.

### Answered by Victor, 2026-10-31 (carry into the PRD as decisions)

- **D33. The staircase is adopted**: "Something's wrong", fixed ways out with 48-hour clocks, a free ruling
  within 5 days with written reasons.
- **D34. Rulings are final.** No appeal step.
- **D35. Abandonment after the start share: the client keeps the start-proof work and loses the 20%**; the
  other 80% is refunded. King Domain explains this plainly before the client pays (the protection screen,
  client trust §6), as the honest limit of what a small platform can cover today. **Both sides' addresses
  are collected** so that serious escalations can be followed up in person by a King Domain agent.
  Follow-ups for the PRD: the address is collected with its purpose stated (NDPA s.27) and can be checked
  through an identity provider (address checks cost about ₦150–₦250, e.g. Bytid); agents' visits need a
  safety procedure. Money rule → ledger for the shareholders' record.
- **D36. A chargeback lost after the talent was paid for approved work is absorbed by King Domain**; the
  talent keeps what they earned and the client is removed. Victor expects these to be rare because every
  payment carries a full receipt and approval record. Money rule → ledger for the record.

## 9. Decisions for Victor

1. **The staircase**: "Something's wrong", then fixed ways out with 48-hour clocks, then a free King Domain
   ruling within 5 days with written reasons. Adopt?
2. **Appeals**: the ledger text says nothing. Is a ruling final, or may either side appeal once, to a
   different admin, within 3 days?
3. **A talent abandons the job after taking the start-proof share (20%).** The client gets the other 80%
   back. For the 20%: (a) King Domain refunds it to the client and recovers it from the talent's future
   earnings, or (b) the client keeps the start-proof work and loses the 20%? Money rule → ledger.
4. **A lost chargeback after the talent was paid for approved work**: (a) King Domain absorbs it and removes
   the client, or (b) it is recovered from the talent? Money rule → ledger.

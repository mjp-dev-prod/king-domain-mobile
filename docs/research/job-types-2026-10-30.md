# Job types: are all remote jobs the same? (research, 2026-10-30)

**Why:** Victor, 2026-10-30: "are all remote jobs the same? is this realistic, the simple stepper". The app
has one job shape and one five-step lifecycle. This is the first research topic for the PRD (see
`project-prd-reset` in the session notes). It proposes; it decides nothing. Decisions are listed in §9.

Tags: 🟢 fact (named source) · 🔵 observed (this codebase) · 🟣 synthesis (several sources agree) ·
🟡 hypothesis · 🔴 unknown.

## 1. Short answer

No. 🟣 Every established marketplace runs at least four job shapes, because the work itself comes in
four shapes: **one delivery** (a logo), **phases** (a website, a brand identity), **ongoing monthly work**
(a social media manager), and **time** (a virtual assistant, open-ended editing). Each shape holds money,
reviews work and ends differently. Nigerian freelancers working without a platform already use all four
(§4). King Domain supports only the first.

## 2. What King Domain has today (🔵)

- `Job`: title, category, free-text description, one budget, `deliveryDays` (1–60).
- `Contract`: one funding, one delivery, client review, then one payout. Agreed rules on top
  (ledger milestone 04): 24 h to pay after award, a delivery date, up to 2 extension requests, 3 days to
  review (silence = approval), up to 2 change rounds, then escalation to an admin.
- The brief is one text box. Nothing records deliverables, quantities, formats, revisions included,
  usage rights or the files the client will provide.
- The lifecycle stepper shows five steps, the same for every job.

## 3. What the established platforms do (🟢)

| Shape | Upwork | Fiverr | Contra |
|---|---|---|---|
| **One delivery, fixed price** | Client funds up front; 14 days to review; silence releases; refund request gives the freelancer 7 days to respond or dispute. | Single-payment order; 3 days to accept or ask for a revision; silence completes. | One-time fixed (escrow); an optional upfront amount is released at once; rest on approval. |
| **Milestones** | Each milestone funded before it starts, one at a time; 14-day review each; milestones can be added mid-contract. | 2–5 milestones; first charged at order, each next one charged after the previous is accepted; 8 days to review; client can stop after any milestone and the freelancer keeps what was approved. | Each milestone funded in full before kickoff, released on approval; 120 h to approve; milestones can be added later. |
| **Recurring / monthly** | "Weekly payment": a fixed recurring amount the client sets, like a retainer. | Subscriptions: each period is a **standalone order**, daily/weekly/monthly, up to 6 months; paid after each delivery; client gives new requirements per period. | Recurring projects, weekly or monthly; fixed invoice billing on a cadence. |
| **Hourly** | Weekly billing; protected only with the desktop time tracker (random screenshots, activity levels, memos); client has Mon–Fri to dispute. | Hours reported **by hand** weekly with a description and files; client has 7 days to approve or dispute, silence approves; minimum 8 h estimate. | One-time hourly (escrow): client pre-pays the **maximum hours × rate**; unused hours are refunded. |

Other details worth keeping:

- **Fiverr "share draft":** a work-in-progress share that does not count as a delivery and does not start
  the review clock. Lets the client see progress without the talent "delivering".
- **Fiverr order requirements:** the client must submit structured requirements before the order starts.
- **Fiverr packages:** up to three priced tiers per service, each with its scope, revisions and delivery
  time, so scope is agreed before money moves.
- **Upwork security hold:** 5 days after release before withdrawal (fraud and chargebacks).

## 4. What Nigerian freelancers do without a platform (🟢)

- **50% up front, 50% on delivery** is the stated norm; 30–50% deposit "is standard and is your single
  best protection" (LegalDoc NG, Naijaonlinebiz, freelancer rate pages). Staged variants exist: 50% / 25%
  on first draft / 25% on final (Billify).
- **Social media management is a monthly retainer**, about ₦50,000–₦250,000 a month depending on
  platforms and post frequency (Naijaonlinebiz 2025; a beginner case study at ₦50,000/month for one
  platform, paid 50% up front and 50% mid-month, collected over OPay).
- **Retainers are packaged** by volume (e.g. 3 posts a week / 5 posts a week + calendar / daily + analytics),
  with a time cap or deliverable cap per month, rollover rules and notice to cancel (African Freelancers).
- **Contract clauses they rely on:** capped revisions with a price for extra rounds; IP transfers only on
  full payment; a kill fee if the client cancels mid-way (UGC template: 25% before shooting, 50% after
  shooting, 75% after first draft); usage rights for creator content (organic, paid ads for 30/90 days, etc.).
- **The pain:** clients ghost, stall payment, keep bargaining, "try you out for free", change scope without
  changing budget. Agreements are often verbal on WhatsApp.

🟣 King Domain's held payment replaces the 50% deposit as the talent's protection. But the deposit also
does a second job the hold doesn't: **it puts money in a broke student's hand before the job ends.** That
matters for the people Balogun described in the 24 Oct meeting. Milestones and an optional upfront
release are the two established ways to do it (§3).

## 5. What goes wrong between clients and freelancers (🟢 research)

- **Vague briefs and scope creep** are the most-cited cause of conflict. Freelancers skip vague posts as a
  waste of time; clients "don't know what they want" (Microsoft Research, CSCW 2022; UBC 2024; Seifried et
  al. 2023).
- **Revision loops**: clients ask for revisions that were never agreed; freelancers comply for fear of a
  bad review (Gussek et al., Electronic Markets 2023).
- **Clients going silent** or **ending work mid-way without explanation**, especially on hourly work, where
  the outcome is agreed gradually (Seifried et al. 2023).
- **Renegotiating scope is emotionally draining**, so freelancers absorb unpaid work instead (UBC 2024).
- Freelancers prefer **hourly where scope is unclear** and **fixed where scope is clear** (CSCW 2022).

🟣 So the brief is part of the protection, not paperwork: a structured brief per job shape (deliverables,
quantity, formats, revisions included, usage rights, files the client provides) is what the review and the
dispute later get judged against.

## 6. Contests: not for King Domain (🟢)

99designs-style contests: the client posts a brief, dozens of designers submit finished work, one is paid.
An analysis of 99designs found about **117 submissions per contest**; total prizes divided by total
submissions came to **about $2.56 per design** (Kennedy 2013, citing 99designs' own figures). AIGA warns
designers about spec work; the International Council of Design tells professionals not to take part and
singles out students and young designers. For students who are short of money, unpaid competition is the
opposite of the product's purpose. Proposed: no contests (§9).

## 7. Payment rails (🟢 Paystack docs)

- Paystack can charge a customer again without them present **only by card or direct debit (Nigeria)**,
  using a stored reusable authorization. **Bank transfer payments cannot recur.**
- Paystack Subscriptions exist (hourly to annual plans) but **a failed subscription charge is not
  retried**, and they suit "value after payment" products.
- 🟣 So a monthly job can't assume automatic charging. Each period should be its own funded cycle (the
  Fiverr subscription model): a reminder before the period, the client funds it by any method, or by a
  stored card or direct debit if they opted in. An unfunded period simply doesn't start.

## 8. Proposal: four job types, each with its own lifecycle (🟣, for the PRD)

| Type | Fits | Money | Lifecycle (what the stepper shows) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **One delivery** | Logo, flyer, one video edit, an article | Whole budget funded after award; released on approval | Agreed → Funded → Brief confirmed → Working (drafts shared) → Delivered → Review (≤2 change rounds) → Approved → Paid |
| **Milestones** | Brand identity, website, a batch of 10 reels, a course video series | Each milestone funded before it starts; released on its approval; client can stop after any approved milestone | The job shows its milestones; each runs Funded → Working → Delivered → Review → Approved → Paid; the next unlocks when funded |
| **Monthly** | Social media management, content writing, ongoing design support, VA by the month | Each month funded before it starts; released after that month's deliveries are approved | Agreed (monthly scope: e.g. 12 posts + calendar) → Month N funded → Working → Month's deliveries → Review → Paid → Month N+1 (renew, change scope, or end with notice) |
| **Hourly** | VA, open-ended editing, research, support | Client pre-funds a weekly cap (hours × rate); unused hours return to the client | Agreed (rate, weekly cap) → Week funded → Hours reported with what was done and files → Client approves or disputes within the window → Paid → next week |

Common to all four:

- A **structured brief** per category and type (deliverables, quantity, formats/dimensions, revisions
  included, usage rights, client-provided files, references, deadline).
- **Drafts** shared without starting the review clock, kept as dated versions (also evidence that the
  talent did the work themselves, which feeds the verification topic).
- The agreed rules (24 h to pay, delivery dates, extensions, 3-day review, 2 change rounds, disputes) apply
  **per deliverable unit**: per job, per milestone, per month, per week. Each needs its own numbers
  confirmed (e.g. is 3 days right for a weekly hours report?).

## 9. Decisions for Victor (and the ledger where it touches money or the marketplace model)

1. **Job types:** adopt the four types in §8 and no contests?
2. **Upfront release:** may part of a funded job (or the first milestone) go to the talent at the start,
   the way Contra allows and the Nigerian 50% norm expects? It helps broke students; the risk is a talent
   taking the upfront amount and disappearing. Money rule → ledger.
3. **Monthly payment:** each month funded by the client on a reminder (any method), with automatic card or
   direct debit only if the client opts in?
4. **Ready-made packages** (a talent publishes "3 reels edited for ₦15,000", Fiverr-style) in addition to
   clients posting jobs? This is the open **marketplace model** decision (reconciliation doc §1). Raise in
   the ledger; not settled here.

### Answered by Victor, 2026-10-30 (carry into the PRD as decisions)

- **D1. Four job types, no contests.** One delivery, milestones, monthly, hourly.
- **D2. Upfront release: yes, but not 50%.** The early money is earned, not given: the talent starts the
  work and submits a **start proof** in a standard format set per job type and category (for example a
  rough cut for video, concept directions for design, the month's content calendar for social media). The
  approved start proof unlocks a smaller share of the held payment. The share, the formats, who approves
  and how fast are to be worked out with the proof-of-work verification topic. Money rule → ledger.
- **D3. Monthly work:** each month funded by the client on a reminder, any method; automatic card or direct
  debit only if the client opts in.
- **D4. Ready-made packages: yes**, alongside client job posts. Marketplace-model decision → post to the
  ledger for the shareholders (team rule).
